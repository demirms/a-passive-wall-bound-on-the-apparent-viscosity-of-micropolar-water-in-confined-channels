(4) Plate channel of Kazoe et al. 2022 [4]: size convention
    Source (Kazoe 2022, J. Anal. Sci. 38, p. 283 and Table 1 p. 283): 'For plate nanochannels, ... D_h and D were used as the representative size D_r of the square- and plate-nanochannels, respectively.'
    Table 1: D_r = 234 +- 3 nm, W = 10000 +- 15 nm, D = 234 +- 3 nm (so D_r = depth D = gap H); resistance C = 12 mu L/(W D^3) (Eq. 3).
    -> in the slit model the gap is H = D = 234 nm (half-gap 117 nm), not 117 nm. The manuscript's D_h = 2H = 468 nm is the conventional hydraulic diameter of a plate (2WD/(W+D) = 457.3 nm for W = 10 um).
    H = D = 234 nm (source's D_r; used in Table 3)                                       BC1 prediction 1.0097 (299 K); measured 1.312 +- 0.067; bound 1.2429
    alternative: D_r read as D_h, i.e. H = 117 nm                                        BC1 prediction 1.0193 (299 K); measured 1.312 +- 0.067; bound 1.2429
    hydraulic diameter 2WD/(W+D) = 457 nm read as H (inconsistent; for completeness)     BC1 prediction 1.0050 (299 K); measured 1.312 +- 0.067; bound 1.2429
    R077 measured 1.312 +- 0.067: above the 299 K bound 1.2429 by 0.069 (1.03 sigma); the lower end 1.245 is above the bound (299 K); 284 K bound 1.185.
    Source's interpretation (Kazoe 2022, p. 286): 'The 1.3 times higher water viscosity in the plate nanochannel with D_r = 234 nm ... is in agreement with the results of previous studies; therefore, this increased viscosity is considered to be due to electroviscous effects.'
    Same paper, same page: for the square 190 nm channel the 10 mM KCl value (3.07e-3 Pa s; text 3.08e-3) is 'almost the same as that of pure water', 'therefore, the electroviscous effect is not a dominant factor' for the square channels.
    Consequence for the manuscript: the plate point R077 is above the bound only marginally (0.07 above, about one error bar) and its source attributes it to electroviscous effects, which the model excludes; the abstract should not list plate channels among the geometries that exceed the bound.
