(4) Capillary-filling and filling-under-pressure sets
    Tas et al. 2004 [2] (Appl. Phys. Lett. 85, 3274; p. 3275): eta_app from the slope a of x = a t^(1/2) with cos(theta) = 0.94 +- 0.02 (low-speed advancing angle, macroscopic measurement) and bulk viscosity at the substrate temperature (+-0.5 C);
      error bars (+-0.11 at 53 nm) 'represent the random errors, mainly ... in the channel height, the contact angle, and in the bulk viscosity'; height 53 +- 6 nm (random, 11 %), 5 % systematic height error is NOT in the bars;
      cos(theta) alone: 2.1 % in eta; eta ~ h cos(theta)/a^2, so a 5 % height error is a 5 % error in eta.
    Haneveld et al. 2008 [3] (J. Appl. Phys. 104, 014309; pp. 014309-3/-4): modified Washburn x = (1/C) sqrt(gamma cos(theta) h_0/(3 eta)) t^(1/2) - b t, the loss term b is fitted, cos(theta) = 0.99 +- 0.01 (macroscopic), C = apparent-viscosity factor; 10.5 +- 0.8 nm channel (7.6 % in h_0, i.e. in C) vs the quoted C = 1.32 +- 0.11 (8.3 %). Each point of their Fig. 5 is one chip (2-3 chips per height); the text does not say whether +-0.11 is a standard deviation or an extreme range.
      The authors list four possible causes of C > 1: dynamic contact angle, electroviscous effect, geometry (wall deformation), and wall-liquid interaction; for 5 nm they suspect channel collapse (p. 014309-4/-5).
    Li et al. 2012 [6] (J. Phys. Chem. Lett.; PDF p. 3): viscosity from the SLOPE and surface tension force gamma cos(theta) from the INTERCEPT of Dx^2/Dt versus the external pressure P_ex (0-2 MPa); a constant dynamic contact angle is assumed and the linearity is taken as evidence; the contact angle therefore does not enter the viscosity if constant.
    Hence only the data of Refs. [1, 4, 5] (pressure-driven flow, viscosity from the resistance or the slope of flow rate versus pressure) are directly comparable with the fully developed Poiseuille flow of the model;
    those of Refs. [2, 3] (spontaneous filling) depend on the contact angle and on the front loss and are kept as supporting data; Ref. [6] (slope method) is intermediate.
